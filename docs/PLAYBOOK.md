## Our strategy — recommendations

Do one important task end to end. A model should interpret something difficult, produce a useful decision aid, and let the user inspect or correct it. A generic chatbot is easy to build but difficult to distinguish.

Choose one person, one moment of difficulty, one input and one useful output. For example, if a relevant theme is drawn, a citizen might photograph a confusing notice and receive a source-backed action checklist. This is an illustrative direction, not an official category or approved entry.

| Official scoring item | Points | Evidence we aim to show |
|---|---:|---|
| Relevance | 5 | Explicit mapping from assigned challenge to user workflow. |
| Impact & Value | 10 | Measured before/after task completion with consented users or clearly labeled pilot tests. |
| UI/UX | 10 | Three coherent screens, clear labels, readable type, loading, retry, usable on phone. |
| Maintainability & Sustainability | 10 | Small codebase, setup instructions, cost per task, quota plan, realistic steward. |
| Functionality | 15 | Real input → real AI → validated output → useful action; failure path included. |
| Technical Innovation | 15 | Necessary AI capability plus source grounding, structured validation or meaningful constraint handling. |
| Originality | 10 | Compare alternatives and explain a specific unmet need. |
| Innovation in Design | 10 | Show a distinctive interaction that removes work for the user. |
| Clarity & Storytelling | 10 | Explain one user's problem and transformation in plain language. |
| Demo & Delivery | 5 | Rehearsed live workflow, clean handoff, accurately labeled backup recording. |

Avoid unnecessary login, dashboards, payment, social feeds and admin panels unless the chosen problem cannot function without them. Improve the core interaction first. Do not claim deployments, integrations, user results or market impact that have not been verified.

## Immediate organizer questions

Ask through your assigned channel; this workspace does not send messages:

1. Are pre-event Flutter/Firebase practice, reusable scaffolds, libraries and prepared assets permitted in the entry, and how should they be disclosed?
2. What is the exact September 24 cutoff, timezone, presentation duration and accepted submission formats?
3. Will there be sponsor-provided models, credits, datasets or required AI tools?
4. May judges use their own Firebase project to reproduce core functionality, or is a hosted endpoint/APK required?
5. What are the consent and data-use expectations for demonstrations?

The predefined sub-theme pool is not listed in the supplied PDF. The public event homepage was checked but provided no extractable current mechanics, so this document does not treat it as confirmation of changed rules.
